> Air consumption also increases in direct proportion to the depth you are diving. The deeper you go, the more gas you use, as described by Boyle’s Law. For example, a diver at two bar absolute (10 m) uses twice as much air as a diver on the surface at one bar absolute. A diver at five bar (40 m) uses five times as much air as a person on the surface. In addition, a diver sharing air with another diver can increase air consumption by a factor of two or more.

> To ensure that the regulator makes breathing easier, you’ll need to dive with your body at a certain angle. When diving, the proper swimming position, also referred to as attitude, is 15 to 20 degrees above horizontal. This attitude allows your mouth and lungs to be horizontal and is the optimal swimming position for breathing easily at depth. In Section 4, we will discuss how this is accomplished with your Buoyancy Compensator (BC).
