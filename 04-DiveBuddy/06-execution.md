> The most common entry is the giant stride from any dive boat or jetty. In a giant stride, with the BC slightly inflated, the diver simply steps off into the water in a long stride, looking forward, not down. The diver secures their mask on the face and primary second stage with one hand and their weight system and information system with the other.

<br>

<p align="left">

> The rotating bezel (scale) represents 360 degrees: 0 or 360 degrees equals magnetic North, 90 degrees equals East, 180 degrees equals South and 270 degrees equals West. Most scales are marked every 5 degrees and numbered every 30 degrees The rotating bezel lets you rotate the witness marks with reference to the North seeking needle. This allows you to set direction.

<br>

<p align="left">
  <img src="https://github.com/thespcrewroy/MARS4740-ScientificDiving/blob/main/assets/4-9.jpg""/>

> Always choose the easiest entry and exit procedures appropriate to the diving conditions. Take precautions in case you accidentally slip back into the water: Keep your mask in place so you can see, your regulator or snorkel in place so you can breathe, and your BC inflated so you can float.

<br>

<p align="left">

> If the initial direction is between 0 degrees and 180 degrees, add 180 degrees to the initial direction to get your return direction. If the initial direction is greater than 180, simply subtract 180 degrees to get the return direction.
